01 · The problem

The middle of the market is the hardest place to buy business software.

Small businesses are served by cheap tools that stop working the moment there is real complexity. Large enterprises have the budget and the internal team to absorb a proper implementation. In between sits everyone else. Too big for the tools, too small to be the vendor’s priority.

So growing businesses get sold projects scoped for companies three times their size, or they keep patching a system that ran out of road two years ago. Both are expensive. One of them is just slower about it.

Spreadsheets are the real system

The software holds the record, but the decisions get made in a workbook on someone’s desktop that only they know how to update.

The accounting package has hit its ceiling

It was the right choice at R10 million turnover. At R80 million it is being asked to do stock, jobs, projects and reporting it was never built for.

Nobody agrees on the numbers

Sales, operations and finance each quote a different figure in the same meeting, because each is reading a different system at a different time.

The owner is the integration layer

The business runs on what one or two people carry in their heads. That is a continuity risk long before it is a technology problem.

A system was bought and never adopted

The licences are being paid for. Half the modules were never configured, and the team quietly went back to the old way of working.

Growth is being absorbed by headcount

Every increase in volume means another pair of hands doing admin, because the process cannot scale without people.

03 · How we work

Four stages. No surprises in stage three.

  1. Assess

    Understand the business first

    We speak to the people doing the work and document the real process, not the one in the procedure manual. Where the time goes, where the errors come from, what it is costing.

    You get: a process and systems map, the problems ranked by what they cost you, and a straight answer on whether new software is warranted.

  2. Design

    Agree what good looks like

    We design the target process and specify what the system has to do, before anyone is asked to sign a licence agreement. Scope, phasing and commercials are fixed here, in writing.

    You get: a recommendation with reasoning, a phased plan, and a proposal with no open-ended time and materials.

  3. Implement

    Deliver in phases that go live

    We start with the part of the business that is hurting most and get it working, rather than configuring everything for a single high-risk cutover. Your team is trained as we go.

    You get: a working system in production, clean migrated data, and people who know how to use it.

  4. Support

    Stay after go-live

    The weeks after go-live decide whether an implementation succeeds. We stay on to resolve issues, roll out what was deferred, and adjust as the business changes.

    You get: a support arrangement sized to your business, and documentation that means you are never locked in.

05 · About us

Everyone knows a guy. We’re the guy that guy calls.

There is already someone your business phones. An IT person, the developer who built the portal, your accountant’s recommended contact. They are usually good at what they do, and every one of them eventually hits something sitting just outside it.

When that happens they do not stop. They say I know a guy. We are the call on the other end of that sentence.

Accountants send us the client whose system no longer fits the business. IT and development people send us the project that turned out to be a process problem rather than a code problem. Vendors send us the implementation that needs someone independent in the room. That is most of how we get work, and it is a standard worth holding: you only get referred by another professional if you make them look good.

SNP Consulting was founded by Henry Marais and Andile Faku to make that referral into something a business can actually contract with. A documented method, a support arrangement, and more than one person who knows how your business works.

A guy is not a business. We kept the trust and added the contract, the method, and someone to phone when it breaks.
